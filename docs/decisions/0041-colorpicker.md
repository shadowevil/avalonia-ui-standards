# 0041 — ColorPicker and ColorView

- **Status:** Agreed, 2026-11-13
- **Decision:**
  - The controls come from Avalonia's separate package `Avalonia.Controls.ColorPicker`, pinned at 12.1.6 (the version matching Avalonia 12.1.6) in `Directory.Packages.props` ([0008](0008-target-versions.md), [0010](0010-solution-layout.md)). The library references it, so apps get it with the library.
  - `StandardsTheme` adds the package's Fluent styles (`avares://Avalonia.Controls.ColorPicker/Themes/Fluent/Fluent.xaml`) as a child right after `FluentTheme`, as Fluent itself is a child, and the Blocks themes for ColorPicker, ColorView, ColorSpectrum, ColorSlider and ColorPreviewer override the package's ([0017](0017-app-usage.md): no extra step for apps). Apps do not add the package's `Fluent.xaml` include themselves: one added after `StandardsTheme` would put Fluent's look back over the library's.
    - **Why:** The package's own resources (the checkerboard brush, its converters) stay available to apps and to the library's templates, and the library's themes win because the theme's own resources are looked up before its children.
  - All five are Blocks ([0004](0004-tier-names.md)), restyled Fluent ([0003](0003-restyle-fluent.md)) with the library's own templates. Fluent's part and element names are kept (`PART_HexTextBox`, `PART_TabControl`, the spectrum's `PART_LayoutRoot`, `PART_SizingPanel`, `PART_SpectrumRectangle`, `PART_SpectrumOverlayRectangle`, `PART_SpectrumEllipse`, `PART_SpectrumOverlayEllipse`, `PART_InputTarget`, `PART_SelectionEllipsePanel`, the sliders' `PART_Track`, `PART_DecreaseButton`, `PART_IncreaseButton`, the previewer's four `PART_Accent…Border`s, and `ColorSpectrum`, `Component1Slider` and the other named sliders and inputs), so the controls' code and app styles find them.
    - **Why:** The colour logic (spectrum bitmaps, slider gradients, hex parsing, accent steps) stays the package's; only the look changes.
  - **The colour chip.** Every shown colour (the picker's chip, palette colours, the previewer) is a squircle over Fluent's checkerboard (`ColorControlCheckeredBackgroundBrush`) with a 1 px hairline edge in the Slider thumb's edge colour ([0031](0031-slider-and-progress.md)).
    - **Why:** The checkerboard shows transparency, as in Fluent. A translucent dark edge separates a colour from a surface of the same colour (a white chip on a white field) on any colour and in both themes, and it is a value the library already has.
  - **The ColorPicker field.** The closed ColorPicker is drawn as the closed ComboBox field ([0028](0028-combobox.md)): its chrome, density height, border, colours, no pressed look, and its square caret zone with `Icons.CaretDown`. The chip fills the rest, sized and inset as a field button ([0027](0027-text-input.md)), with the field button's corner radius. Fluent's minimum width 64 is kept. An app's `Content` replaces the chip, as in Fluent. Keyboard focus shows the focus ring ([0019](0019-focus-ring.md)); pointer focus shows nothing.
    - **Why:** The picker shows a value and opens a chooser for it, as ComboBox, DatePicker and CalendarDatePicker do, so it takes the field look they share rather than the DropDownButton's Standard button look (Fluent's, and WinUI's sample) or Fluent's chevron. The field-button inset and radius make the chip concentric with the field, like the clear and spin buttons.
  - **The flyout.** The picker's button is Fluent's `DropDownButton` with a `Flyout` holding the ColorView, so its presenter is the library's glass flyout, with its radius, padding, overlay layer and fade ([0036](0036-menus-and-flyouts.md)). It opens below the field, left edges aligned, `SpacingXS` apart; Avalonia flips it when there is no room. Fluent's zero-padding class and its placement above the field are dropped.
    - **Why:** It is the glass overlay rule of 0036, and below the field is where the ComboBox and CalendarDatePicker drop-downs open, so the field's overlays open in one place.
  - **The ColorView.** Tabs over pages, then the previewer. The view has no surface of its own; it sits on its container (the flyout's glass, or a panel), as tab content does ([0033](0033-tabs-and-expander.md)).
    - The mode switch (spectrum, palette, components) is Fluent's `TabControl` with the library's tab look: icon headers (`Icons.Drop`, `Icons.SquaresFour`, `Icons.SlidersHorizontal`) at the Icon density size, the sliding accent underline and the hairline, left-aligned. Each tab has a tooltip and an automation name (see Strings). Fluent's grey tab band and equal-width tabs are dropped.
      - **Why:** 0033 keeps tabs for switching between content areas and the segmented control for choosing a value of a setting ([0032](0032-segmented-control.md)); the three modes are three views of the same colour, not a setting. Icons, as in Fluent and macOS's colour panel, keep the strip short; an icon alone does not name the view, so the tooltip and automation name do (the 0032 icon-segment rule).
    - The pages sit flush with the tab strip's left and right edges (without 0033's content padding), `SpacingM` below it. Every page is 256 px high (the spectrum's side), and the view's width is the spectrum plus a vertical slider either side, `SpacingM` apart: 320 at Normal, 312 at Compact. Fluent's fixed 350 × 338 is dropped.
      - **Why:** Switching tabs never changes the view's size, the sizes sit on the 4 px grid ([0013](0013-spacing-and-density.md)), and the pages line up with the previewer below them.
    - The spectrum page: the third-component slider, the spectrum and the alpha slider, `SpacingM` apart, as in Fluent.
    - The palette page: the library ListBox (padding `SpacingXS`) over Fluent's palette, in a uniform grid of the palette's columns. Each colour is a cell at least 24 px high (20 at Compact, the field-button size) holding its chip `SpacingXS` in, with the chip's corner radius the cell's less that inset (4). Hover and press are the list item fills behind the chip ([0024](0024-lists-and-scrolling.md)); the selected cell has a 2 px accent ring (`AccentFillColor`) on its edge with a 2 px gap to the chip, in `AccentButtonBackgroundDisabled` when the view is disabled; keyboard focus shows the focus ring outside the cell. Each chip has the colour's display name as its tooltip and automation name, as in Fluent.
      - Keys, as on a grid: Left and Right move along a row (swapped right to left), Up and Down along a column, Home and End to the first and last colour; movement stops at the grid's edges, and the navigation keys are always handled. The colour under the keyboard is selected.
      - **Why:** The list item fills are the library's hover and press. A ring rather than a fill marks the selection because a fill would cover the colour. The ring sits inside the cell and the focus ring outside it, so selection and keyboard focus on the same cell read as two marks, not one ring drawn twice. Fluent's contrast-coloured border is dropped for the accent. A ListBox has no arrow navigation in a uniform grid, so the palette handles the keys itself, as the segmented control does (0032); selection follows the keyboard there too.
    - The components page: the colour model as a SegmentedControl and the hex value as a TextInput with a `#` in its side padding, `SpacingXS` before the text, side by side; then one row per component: its letter in a 16 px column, `SpacingS` before a NumericUpDown (`Width` = 64) with no spin buttons (as in Fluent), then the component's colour slider. The letters and the `#` are in the placeholder colour (`TextControlPlaceholderForeground`), and the disabled text colour when disabled. Rows are `SpacingM` apart, from the top of the page.
      - **Why:** The model is a setting with two values, the case for the segmented control (0032). The inputs are the library's field Blocks, so they line up and show focus like every other field. Fluent's joined label boxes with squared corners are dropped: the letters label the fields as text, and the fields keep their own squircles.
  - **The spectrum.** The box spectrum is a squircle with `CornerRadiusControl` and the hairline edge; the ring shape keeps Fluent's ellipse, with the same edge. The selector is not clipped, so it shows in full at the edges.
  - **The colour thumb.** The spectrum's selector and every ColorSlider's thumb are one design: the Slider thumb's size, shadow, edge colour and hover and press growth ([0031](0031-slider-and-progress.md)), drawn as a white ring 2 px wide between two 1 px hairline edges, with an open centre that shows the colour under it. Disabled, it is the same white ring at opacity 0.4. Fluent's large selector under a touch press (48 px) is kept. Keyboard focus shows the focus ring around the thumb.
    - **Why:** A solid white thumb, as on the Slider, would hide the colour it picks; a ring is the colour selector on macOS, iOS and WinUI. The edges and shadow make the white ring read on white as well as on dark colours, so Fluent's switch between a black and a white selector by luminance is dropped. A grey disabled ring would vanish over grey parts of a gradient; a faded white ring stays visible over any. The library had no disabled opacity, so 0.4 is this thumb's value. The focus ring goes around the thumb because the arrow keys move it, as on the Slider.
  - **The colour sliders.** A ColorSlider's track is a full pill as thick as the thumb, showing the slider's gradient over the checkerboard with the hairline edge; the thumb runs along it.
    - **Why:** The gradient is the slider's information, so the track must be thick enough to show it; 0031's track is a line for a value, not a colour. The pill shape is 0031's. As thick as the thumb is Fluent's proportion, and the thumb's hover growth then shows past the track.
  - **The previewer.** The colour as a chip with `CornerRadiusControl`, as high as a control row (32 or 24), with Fluent's four accent chips (two darker, two lighter; clicking one picks it) as square chips either side, `SpacingXS` apart. Fluent's drop shadow is dropped.
    - **Why:** Chips are the library's colour swatch; a row height lines the previewer up with the inputs above it. Shadows in the library mark things to grab (thumbs), and a preview is not one.
  - **Strings.** Every shown or spoken string of the view is a string resource in the theme's dictionary with an English default, looked up dynamically, so an app overrides one by putting the same key in its own resources (for example `Application.Resources`), which are found before the theme's:

    | Key | Default | Used for |
    |---|---|---|
    | `ColorViewSpectrumText`, `ColorViewPaletteText`, `ColorViewComponentsText` | Spectrum, Palette, Components | the tabs' tooltips and automation names; the spectrum's and the palette's automation names |
    | `ColorViewRgbText`, `ColorViewHsvText` | RGB, HSV | the colour model segments |
    | `ColorViewHexText` | Hexadecimal colour | the hex box's automation name |
    | `ColorViewRedText`, `ColorViewGreenText`, `ColorViewBlueText`, `ColorViewHueText`, `ColorViewSaturationText`, `ColorViewValueText`, `ColorViewAlphaText` | Red, Green, Blue, Hue, Saturation, Value, Alpha | the automation names of the component boxes and sliders (by the colour model), the spectrum's third-component slider (by the component it sets) and the alpha sliders |
    | `ColorViewRedLetter` … `ColorViewAlphaLetter` (the same seven) | R, G, B, H, S, V, A | the component letters |

    - **Why:** Apps in other languages can name every part without replacing a template, and the boxes and sliders are announced by what they set (Red, Hue), not by Fluent's "Component1".
  - Motion: the field's chrome, the palette cells and the thumbs use the button family's `MotionFast` transitions, none under reduce motion ([0016](0016-motion.md)); the tabs and the segmented control keep their own motion.
  - The catalog's ColorPicker page uses `Icons.Eyedropper` (Phosphor `eyedropper`), added under the rule of [0026](0026-icon.md).
- **Known for later:** Every colour surface keeps its full colour when disabled: the spectrum, the slider gradients, the palette chips, the previewer and the picker's chip (only the field, its border and caret, the text, the thumbs and the selection ring take their disabled looks). Dimming them would need a value the library does not have ([0007](0007-restyle-scope.md)).
- **Why:** The colour controls join the field, tab, list and slider families: the same field, overlay, focus ring and motion, with the colour logic left to the package.
