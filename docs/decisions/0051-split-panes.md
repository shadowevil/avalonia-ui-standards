# 0051 — Split panes and panel stacks

- **Status:** Agreed, 2026-11-16
- **Decision:**
  - Layout: `StandardsWindow` ([0022](0022-window-layout.md)) has two more content slots, `Inspector` and `BottomPanel`.
    - The inspector is a second glass panel (`GlassBorder`, radius 16, [0015](0015-glass.md)) to the right of the content panel, the full height of the content area. The bottom panel is a glass panel of its own directly under the content panel only; the inspector stays full height.
    - The gaps between panels are `SpacingS` (8 px, [0013](0013-spacing-and-density.md)), and each gap is the resize handle: a splitter in the GridSplitter look and grip of [0039](0039-separator-and-gridsplitter.md) filling the gap.
    - With a slot empty, or its panel closed, the layout is exactly the content panel alone: the content area keeps `SpacingS` from the window's sides and bottom ([0022](0022-window-layout.md) Values).
    - **Why:** One inspector beside the content and one panel under it is the layout of Xcode, Pages and VS Code. A full-height inspector keeps its column steady while the bottom panel opens and closes. Glass is the panel material of [0022](0022-window-layout.md), so the new panels read as siblings of the content panel. A handle that fills the gap needs no extra room and uses the existing splitter look.
  - Each panel pads its content by `SpacingM` (12, as the content panel) and gives it the panel's size; the panel does not scroll it.
    - **Why:** Content that scrolls itself (a tab group, a list, a console) would lose its height and its virtualisation in a scrolled panel, as noted for `IsContentScrollable` in [0022](0022-window-layout.md); a `PanelStack` scrolls its own cards.
  - Sizes (Values below). The properties `IsInspectorOpen` (default true), `InspectorWidth`, `IsBottomPanelOpen` (default true) and `BottomPanelHeight` are two-way by default.
    - `InspectorWidth` is kept between the inspector's minimum and maximum. `BottomPanelHeight` is kept at or above the bottom panel's minimum; the panel shows at most (content area height − 8) / 2, so the content panel is always at least as tall as the bottom panel, and a taller asked height is kept for a taller window.
    - The minimums and maximums are internal values, not properties, and so is the 1248 threshold.
    - **Why:** Two-way properties let an app keep the layout between runs. 240 matches the sidebar's expanded width ([0034](0034-sidebar.md)) and the web proposal, below AppKit's 270 inspector default, so the inspector and sidebar balance and the content keeps the most room. The bottom panel's maximum keeps the document the larger of the two. Fixed limits keep every app's panels the same; the sidebar's widths are fixed the same way.
  - Short windows: while the content area is shorter than the bottom panel's minimum plus the gap plus an equally tall content panel (2 × 96 + 8 = 200), the bottom panel hides from the layout. `IsBottomPanelOpen` keeps its value, and the panel returns when the window grows. The change applies at once. Meanwhile `ToggleBottomPanel()` does nothing and `BottomPanelMenuItem` is disabled (reading "Show Bottom Panel"), so its gesture does nothing either, as `FullScreenMenuItem` is disabled while the window cannot be maximised.
    - **Why:** Below 200 the bottom panel could only show by making the content panel shorter than it, or shorter than its own minimum; hiding it keeps the content first, as the inspector does in a narrow window, and keeping the flag brings it back without the user doing anything. The panel cannot show there, so a toggle would only lose the kept setting.
  - Resizing: dragging a handle resizes its panel within its limits. Dragging below the panel's minimum collapses the panel (sets its open flag false) and ends the drag; reopened, it comes back at its last size. There is no double-click gesture. The arrow keys step the size (`KeyboardIncrement`) within the minimum and maximum, without collapsing. Escape during a drag puts back the size asked before the drag (`BottomPanelHeight` as it was, even where a shorter height showed).
    - **Why:** Collapsing below the minimum is how Xcode and VS Code close their panels by drag, without a second gesture to learn. Arrow keys and Escape are GridSplitter's keyboard behaviour ([0039](0039-separator-and-gridsplitter.md)), kept because the handle sizes the panel itself instead of a grid track.
  - Narrow windows: the inspector shows docked from a window width (the frame's, as for the sidebar) of 1248. Below it the inspector hides from the layout, and toggling it opens it as a glass overlay at the right edge, `InspectorWidth` wide and the full height of the content area, over the content panel and the bottom panel. This is the "inspector hides first" collapse stage of [0022](0022-window-layout.md).
    - **Why (1248):** it is the sidebar's expanded threshold plus the inspector's default width (1008 + 240), so the content panel keeps the room it has at the sidebar's threshold when the inspector docks, and the inspector hides before the sidebar collapses.
    - **Why (overlay):** it matches the sidebar's overlay stage ([0034](0034-sidebar.md)): the content does not reflow in a narrow window, and overlays are glass.
  - The overlay:
    - Opening it moves keyboard focus to the first focusable control in it. Closing it gives focus back to what had it when it opened, if focus was in the overlay.
      - **Why:** The sidebar overlay focuses its row on opening, so the keyboard user who opened it with the shortcut can work in it at once; focus left in a closed overlay would be lost, and focus elsewhere is where the user put it.
    - It closes on Escape, on a click outside it (that click is not passed on), on the toggle and when the window crosses the threshold, as the sidebar overlay does.
    - Presses inside a popup opened from it (a drop-down, a menu, a flyout) are the popup's, and while any popup is open Escape closes the popup first, not the overlay.
      - **Why:** A drop-down or flyout opens in the window's overlay layer, outside the inspector; counting a press there as outside, or closing the overlay on Escape while a control's own popup is open (a ComboBox keeps focus on itself while its drop-down is open), would close the inspector under the control in use.
  - Motion ([0016](0016-motion.md)): the overlay slides in from the right edge and out again over `MotionNormal` with the motion easing, as the sidebar overlay's toggle animates; showing and hiding the docked inspector and the bottom panel slides them in from and out to their edges with the same timing, the content panel growing or shrinking with them. A panel stays drawn until it has slid out. Changes from resizing (crossing the threshold, a short window) and new slot content apply at once. Under reduce motion everything is instant. The transitions are fixed theme objects.
    - **Why:** The same motion as the sidebar overlay keeps the window's panels one family; sliding shows where a panel went. As with the sidebar, an animation during a live resize would lag behind the pointer.
  - Toggles: `ToggleInspector()` (in a narrow window it opens or closes the overlay) and `ToggleBottomPanel()` on `StandardsWindow`, each doing nothing while its slot is empty. Two `NativeMenuItem` Modules, like `FullScreenMenuItem` ([0049](0049-full-screen.md)), are the View menu's items: `InspectorMenuItem` ("Show Inspector", "Hide Inspector" while the inspector shows, docked or as the overlay) and `BottomPanelMenuItem` ("Show Bottom Panel", "Hide Bottom Panel" while the bottom panel shows). Their headers come from the string resources `InspectorShowHeader`, `InspectorHideHeader`, `BottomPanelShowHeader` and `BottomPanelHideHeader`; the window finds them at any depth in its menu; they are disabled while the slot is empty (the bottom panel's also in a short window, above).
    - Gestures: in the macOS layout Opt+Cmd+I and Opt+Cmd+J; in the Windows layout Ctrl+Alt+I and Ctrl+J. They follow `ResolvedLayoutMode`, and before an item has a window, the layout of the OS it runs on; this is an exception to [0022](0022-window-layout.md)'s rule that shortcuts follow the OS. The shortcuts work through the menu: the system menu bar where the menu is exported, otherwise the window's menu shortcuts ([0044](0044-menu-bar.md)), as other items do.
    - **Why:** Pages and Keynote show and hide the inspector with Opt+Cmd+I, and VS Code toggles its bottom panel with Ctrl+J; Ctrl+Alt+I is Opt+Cmd+I's Windows form, as Ctrl+J is for Opt+Cmd+J. Menu items make the commands discoverable and translatable, as the full screen item is. The gestures follow the layout so the Windows layout shows and accepts the Windows gestures when checked on a Mac.
  - `PanelStack` (a Module, [0004](0004-tier-names.md)) is an items control of `PanelStackItem` (`Header`, `Icon`, `IsExpanded` and content; an `Expander`). Each item is a solid card with a collapsible title strip in the restyled Expander look of [0033](0033-tabs-and-expander.md): caret, then the optional icon `SpacingS` after it, then the header `SpacingS` after the icon. Cards are stacked `SpacingS` apart. Other items are wrapped in a `PanelStackItem`.
    - The stack scrolls vertically when its cards are taller than it.
      - **Why:** The inspector panel does not scroll its content (above), and open cards can be taller than a short window.
    - Tab groups are a `TabControl` placed in an item; there is no new tab control. There is no icon-rail collapse (later, with the tool palette).
    - **Why:** Inspector sections are groups that open and close, which is what the Expander already is, with its card, caret, reveal and focus ring; cards inside a glass panel are solid ([0015](0015-glass.md)). A `TabControl` already gives tab groups the agreed tab look.
  - Glass: the inspector and bottom panel are first-level glass siblings of the content panel. They read the window's shared backdrop sample and take part in `BackdropVisibleRegion` as the content panel does (opaque content in them is skipped and cut out; solid panels with an opaque tint are cut out) ([0015](0015-glass.md)). Glass that is not effectively visible (a closed panel or overlay) reads nothing.
    - **Why:** A closed panel keeps its last bounds; without this its area would still be copied and blurred every frame for glass nobody sees.
  - Not in this step: floating panels, re-docking and layout serialization. Dock.Avalonia is not used.
    - **Why:** Dock.Avalonia's floating windows would not use the `StandardsWindow` frame, so its panels would leave the library's window look; building the two slots on `StandardsWindow` keeps the frame, glass and menus. This is reconsidered at the floating step.
  - Web ([0045](0045-web-library.md)): the inspector and bottom panel are desktop only for now.
  - Values:

    | | Minimum | Default | Maximum |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Inspector width | 200 | 240 | 400 |
    | Bottom panel height | 96 | 200 | (content area height − 8) / 2 |
    | Content area height that holds the bottom panel | 200 | | |
    | Gap between panels (the handle) | | `SpacingS` (8) | |
    | Inspector docks from window width | | 1248 | |

- **Why:** Apps get the inspector and bottom panel that document and tool apps expect, in the window's own frame and glass, sized, collapsing and moving the way the sidebar does.
