# 0051 — Split panes and panel stacks

- **Status:** Agreed, 2026-11-16
- **Decision:**
  - Layout: `StandardsWindow` ([0022](0022-window-layout.md)) has two more content slots, `Inspector` and `BottomPanel`.
    - The inspector is a second glass panel (`GlassBorder`, radius 16, [0015](0015-glass.md)) to the right of the content panel, the full height of the content area. The bottom panel is a glass panel of its own directly under the content panel only; the inspector stays full height.
    - The gaps between panels are `SpacingS` (8 px, [0013](0013-spacing-and-density.md)), and each gap is the resize handle: a splitter in the GridSplitter look and grip of [0039](0039-separator-and-gridsplitter.md) filling the gap, with the arrow keys stepping the size (`KeyboardIncrement`, within the minimum and maximum) and Escape during a drag putting the size back.
    - Each panel pads its content by `SpacingM` (12, as the content panel) and gives it the panel's size; it does not scroll it.
    - With a slot empty, or its panel closed, the layout is exactly the content panel alone: the content area keeps `SpacingS` from the window's sides and bottom ([0022](0022-window-layout.md) Values).
    - **Why:** One inspector beside the content and one panel under it is the layout of Xcode, Pages and VS Code. A full-height inspector keeps its column steady while the bottom panel opens and closes. Glass is the panel material of [0022](0022-window-layout.md), so the new panels read as siblings of the content panel. A handle that fills the gap needs no extra room and uses the existing splitter look.
    - **Why (no scrolling):** content that scrolls itself (a tab group, a list, a console) would lose its height and its virtualisation in a scrolled panel, as noted for `IsContentScrollable` in [0022](0022-window-layout.md); a `PanelStack` scrolls its own cards.
  - Sizes (values below), with the properties `IsInspectorOpen` (default true), `InspectorWidth`, `IsBottomPanelOpen` (default true) and `BottomPanelHeight`, all two-way by default so apps can keep them. The widths and heights are coerced into their limits (the bottom panel to its minimum; it shows at most half the content area's height and keeps the asked height for a taller window). The limits are not properties.
    - Dragging a handle below the panel's minimum collapses the panel (sets its open flag false) and ends the drag; reopened, it comes back at its last size. There is no double-click gesture.
    - **Why:** 240 matches the sidebar's expanded width ([0034](0034-sidebar.md)) and the web proposal, below AppKit's 270 inspector default, so the inspector and sidebar balance and the content keeps the most room. The bottom panel's half-height maximum keeps the content panel at least as tall as it. Collapsing below the minimum is how Xcode and VS Code close their panels by drag, without a second gesture to learn.
  - Narrow windows: the inspector shows docked from a window width (the frame's, as for the sidebar) of 1248. Below it the inspector hides from the layout, and toggling it opens it as a glass overlay at the right edge, `InspectorWidth` wide and the full height of the content area, over the content panel and the bottom panel. The overlay closes on Escape, on a click outside it (that click is not passed on), on the toggle and when the window crosses the threshold; closing gives focus back to what had it when it opened if focus was in the overlay. Presses and keys inside a popup opened from it (a drop-down, a menu) are the popup's. This is the "inspector hides first" collapse stage of [0022](0022-window-layout.md).
    - **Why (1248):** it is the sidebar's expanded threshold plus the inspector's default width (1008 + 240), so the content panel keeps the room it has at the sidebar's threshold when the inspector docks, and the inspector hides before the sidebar collapses.
    - **Why (overlay):** it matches the sidebar's overlay stage ([0034](0034-sidebar.md)): the content does not reflow in a narrow window, and overlays are glass.
  - Toggles: `ToggleInspector()` (in a narrow window it opens or closes the overlay) and `ToggleBottomPanel()` on `StandardsWindow`, each doing nothing while its slot is empty. Two `NativeMenuItem` Modules, like `FullScreenMenuItem` ([0049](0049-full-screen.md)), are the View menu's items: `InspectorMenuItem` ("Show Inspector", "Hide Inspector" while the inspector shows, docked or as the overlay) and `BottomPanelMenuItem` ("Show Bottom Panel", "Hide Bottom Panel"). Their headers come from the string resources `InspectorShowHeader`, `InspectorHideHeader`, `BottomPanelShowHeader` and `BottomPanelHideHeader`; the window finds them at any depth in its menu; they are disabled while the slot is empty.
    - Gestures: in the macOS layout Opt+Cmd+I and Opt+Cmd+J; in the Windows layout Ctrl+Alt+I and Ctrl+J. They follow `ResolvedLayoutMode`, and before an item has a window, the layout of the OS it runs on. The shortcuts work through the menu: the system menu bar where the menu is exported, otherwise the window's menu shortcuts ([0044](0044-menu-bar.md)), as other items do.
    - **Why:** Pages and Keynote show and hide the inspector with Opt+Cmd+I, and VS Code toggles its bottom panel with Ctrl+J; Ctrl+Alt+I is Opt+Cmd+I's Windows form, as Ctrl+J is for Opt+Cmd+J. Menu items make the commands discoverable and translatable, as the full screen item is.
  - `PanelStack` (a Module, [0004](0004-tier-names.md)) is an items control of `PanelStackItem` (`Header`, `Icon`, `IsExpanded` and content; an `Expander`). Each item is a solid card with a collapsible title strip in the restyled Expander look of [0033](0033-tabs-and-expander.md): caret, then the optional icon `SpacingS` after it, then the header `SpacingS` after the icon. Cards are stacked `SpacingS` apart, and the stack scrolls vertically when its cards are taller than it. Other items are wrapped in a `PanelStackItem`.
    - Tab groups are a `TabControl` placed in an item; there is no new tab control. There is no icon-rail collapse (later, with the tool palette).
    - **Why:** Inspector sections are groups that open and close, which is what the Expander already is, with its card, caret, reveal and focus ring; cards inside a glass panel are solid ([0015](0015-glass.md)). A `TabControl` already gives tab groups the agreed tab look.
  - Glass: the inspector and bottom panel are first-level glass siblings of the content panel. They read the window's shared backdrop sample and take part in `BackdropVisibleRegion` as the content panel does (opaque content in them is skipped and cut out; solid panels with an opaque tint are cut out) ([0015](0015-glass.md)). A hidden panel or overlay reads nothing.
  - Not in this step: floating panels, re-docking and layout serialization. Dock.Avalonia is not used.
    - **Why:** Dock.Avalonia's floating windows would not use the `StandardsWindow` frame, so its panels would leave the library's window look; building the two slots on `StandardsWindow` keeps the frame, glass and menus. This is reconsidered at the floating step.
  - Values:

    | | Minimum | Default | Maximum |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Inspector width | 200 | 240 | 400 |
    | Bottom panel height | 96 | 200 | half the content area's height |
    | Gap between panels (the handle) | | `SpacingS` (8) | |
    | Inspector docks from window width | | 1248 | |

- **Why:** Apps get the inspector and bottom panel that document and tool apps expect, in the window's own frame and glass, sized and collapsing the way the sidebar does.
