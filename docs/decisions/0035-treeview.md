# 0035 — TreeView

- **Status:** Proposed, 2026-11-13 (click modes, lines and the content pill added)
- **Decision:**
  - TreeView and TreeViewItem are restyled Fluent Blocks ([0003](0003-restyle-fluent.md), [0004](0004-tier-names.md)). Selection and keyboard stay Fluent's: Up and Down move between visible rows; Right expands a collapsed item, then moves to its first child; Left collapses an expanded item, then moves to its parent; Enter toggles.
    - **Why:** Fluent's tree keyboard model is the Windows and macOS outline model; only the look changes.
  - Which clicks on a row expand and collapse it are two settings, the attached properties `TreeViewOptions.ExpandOn` and `TreeViewOptions.CollapseOn` ([0017](0017-app-usage.md)), each a `[Flags]` `TreeViewClickAction`: `None`, `SingleClick`, `DoubleClick`, or both. Both default to `DoubleClick`, so a single click only selects. They are set on the TreeView and inherited by its items; an item overrides them for itself and its descendants.
    - **Why:** Double click to expand and collapse is the Fluent and Windows default. Apps whose trees are navigation (a single click opens a folder) need single click, and expand and collapse are separate because some trees expand on one click but collapse only deliberately.
  - Row click rules:
    - A click anywhere on the row counts (the indent, the pill and its empty part), except where the press is handled inside the row. The caret handles its own press and toggles on each of its own clicks, whatever the settings; a double click on the caret is two clicks and toggles twice.
    - One gesture toggles at most once: when the first click of a double click toggled the row (the setting includes `SingleClick`), the double click that follows does nothing, even if the other setting includes `DoubleClick`. So with both enabled for an action, a double click acts as one click.
    - Rows without children ignore the settings, and their double clicks stay unhandled for the app (to open an item). A click that toggles is handled.
    - Clicks with Ctrl, Shift or Cmd ignore the settings: they extend or change the selection.
    - Clicks whose press the row's content handles itself (a button in a header) are ignored, single or double.
    - **Why:** One click toggling and the double click undoing it would make "both" useless; treating the double click as already done makes every setting predictable. The whole row is the target so the setting does not depend on hitting the text (Fluent toggles only on a double click on the header's text).
  - The TreeView is transparent with padding 4 (`SpacingXS`) and no focus visual of its own, as the ListBox ([0024](0024-lists-and-scrolling.md)).
  - Each item's row is the tree's full width, inset 4 from its edges, and holds the indent, the caret and the content pill. The pill is the list item design of [0024](0024-lists-and-scrolling.md): a squircle (`CornerRadiusControl`), 32 high at Normal and 24 at Compact, list-medium hover, base-medium-low press, the accent fill with white text when selected, the focus ring around it ([0019](0019-focus-ring.md)). It runs from the end of the caret's box to the row's right edge, so its right edges align across levels.
    - **Why:** The carets and every node line stay visible on hovered, pressed, selected and focused rows: a full-width pill would cover them. Ending every pill at the row's right edge, rather than hugging the label (content plus padding), keeps the selection one aligned column on the right whatever the label lengths, as in the list design.
  - Hover and press are the whole row's, not only the pill's and not the item's: the pill lights while the pointer is anywhere on the row, and a click anywhere on it selects. An item's bounds include its children's rows, so hovering or pressing a child does not light its parent.
    - **Why:** The row stays as easy to hit as a full-width row, though only the content is painted.
  - Row content: the inset (4 at Normal, 2 at Compact; the caret's own box already insets its glyph), the indent, the caret, then the pill. Inside the pill the header has padding 8 left and 12 right at Normal, 8 and 8 at Compact.
  - The indent is one caret width per level: 20 px at Normal, 16 at Compact (the Icon sizes; [0026](0026-icon.md)). A child's caret starts where its parent's caret ends.
    - **Why:** On the 4 px grid, proportional to the row at each density, and each level steps by exactly one caret, so the hierarchy reads with or without lines.
  - The caret is `Icons.CaretRight` in an `Icon` at the density size, in the text colour, dimmed on a disabled row; it lies outside the pill, so it keeps that colour on a selected row. It turns to point down when expanded, over `MotionNormal` with the motion easing, instantly under reduce motion ([0016](0016-motion.md)). It is not a Tab stop. Items without children keep the caret's room, empty, so sibling headers line up.
    - **Why:** The turn shows the change of state without moving anything; `MotionNormal` is the duration for toggles ([0016](0016-motion.md)).
  - Node lines are an option, the attached property `TreeViewOptions.ShowLines` ([0017](0017-app-usage.md)), set on the TreeView and inherited by its items. The default is true. A child's elbow shows when lines are on for both the child and its parent, so an item's value turns lines on or off for its subtree; the parent's column still passes through a child whose lines are off.
    - **Why:** The lines show at a glance which parent each row belongs to, which indentation alone loses in deep or long trees; with the content pill they stay visible in every row state. Apps that want the plain list look turn them off.
  - The lines are solid 1 px hairlines in the border colour (`BaseLow`, the Separator's line, [0039](0039-separator-and-gridsplitter.md)):
    - Under each expanded parent with children, a vertical line runs down its caret's centre column, from the bottom of the caret's box through its children's rows.
    - Each child gets an elbow from that line into its row: a rounded corner of radius 4 (`SpacingXS`, a quarter circle), then a level line at the row's centre that ends `SpacingXS` before the child's caret glyph, or on a row without children `SpacingXS` before its pill.
    - The column stops at the last child's elbow; it carries on past every other child, through that child's own expanded rows.
    - Collapsed children are hidden, so they have no lines; lines follow expand and collapse, and items added, removed, moved or replaced.
    - The lines sit on the caret columns, one indent (one caret width: 20 Normal, 16 Compact) per level, and are snapped to device pixels: whole device pixels thick (1 at scale 1, 2 at scale 2), so they stay crisp at any scale, and are redrawn when the window's scale changes.
    - **Why:** One line language with the separators and borders; the rounded elbow follows the library's continuous corners instead of the square elbows of older trees; ending short of the caret or pill keeps the line from touching content.
  - The lines are drawn under the rows and end before the pills, so no row state covers them.
  - Children appear and disappear at once on expand and collapse; only the caret animates.
- **Why:** The tree reuses the list's row, shape, colours and motion, so it fits beside every other list in the library.
